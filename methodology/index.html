Abstract: Modern financial technology platforms increasingly delegate analytical, amortization, and ledger workflows to client-side runtimes. However, default execution in ECMAScript engines depends upon IEEE-754 double-precision floating-point arithmetic (binary64), which produces representation drift in recurring decimal fractions. In statutory double-entry accounting and long-term amortized debt obligations, this drift compounds non-linearly, resulting in terminal balance mismatches and audit failures.

This methodology standard formalizes the computational architecture of FinEngine (v0.3.0), an open-source framework developed under the Centre for Fintech and Strategic Business Research (CFSBR). FinEngine implements deterministic, integer-scaled monetary arithmetic, establishes native sub-unit primitives for the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT Poisha), and specifies zero-knowledge client-side algorithms for reducing-balance loan amortization, non-periodic cash flow solvers (XIRR), and debt-burden ratio (DBR) stress modeling.
Section 1

The Numerical Challenge: IEEE-754 binary64 Floating-Point Drift

1.1 Structural Root of Fractional Inaccuracy

Standard JavaScript runtimes implement numbers under the IEEE-754 standard for binary floating-point arithmetic (binary64). Memory allocation comprises:

  • 1 sign bit ($s$)
  • 11 biased exponent bits ($e$)
  • 52 explicit significand bits ($m$, yielding 53 bits of precision with the implicit leading bit)

Numerical evaluation follows:

$$V = (-1)^s \times (1.m)_2 \times 2^{e - 1023}$$

Because fractional evaluation relies on sums of negative powers of two ($2^{-1} = 0.5$, $2^{-2} = 0.25$, $2^{-3} = 0.125$, ...), standard decimal values such as $0.1$ and $0.2$ generate non-terminating binary fractions:

$$0.1_{10} = 0.00011001100110011..._2$$
$$0.2_{10} = 0.0011001100110011..._2$$

Truncation at 53 bits produces standard floating-point representation drift:

$$0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004 \neq 0.3$$

1.2 Compounding Propagation in Loan Amortization

In multi-period loan repayment schedules, balance recursion is expressed as:

$$B_t = B_{t-1}(1 + r) - EMI$$

Where:

  • $B_t$ is the closing principal balance at period $t$.
  • $r$ is the periodic interest rate.
  • $EMI$ is the equated monthly installment.

Under unmitigated floating-point arithmetic, representation errors accumulate over discrete repayment horizons ($n \ge 60$ months):

$$B_t^* = B_t + \sum_{k=1}^t \epsilon_k (1 + r)^{t-k}$$

This cumulative drift causes the terminal principal balance ($B_n$) to fail to liquidate cleanly to zero ($B_n \neq 0.00$), creating statutory reconciliation discrepancies across accounting records.

Section 2

Deterministic Arithmetic and BDT Primitives

FinEngine replaces floating-point calculations with integer-scaled arithmetic executed within an immutable sub-unit space.

2.1 Integer Scaling Transformation

All financial inputs are mapped to their lowest statutory atomic sub-unit. For the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT), the atomic sub-unit is the Poisha ($1 \text{ BDT} = 100 \text{ Poisha}$).

The scaling function $f: \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is defined as:

$$V_{\text{scaled}} = \left\lfloor V \cdot 10^k + \frac{1}{2} \text{sgn}(V) \right\rfloor$$

Where:

  • $V$ is the decimal currency figure.
  • $k$ is the sub-unit scale exponent ($k = 2$ for BDT).
  • $\text{sgn}(V)$ maintains directional parity for negative entries.

All subsequent additions, subtractions, and balance tracking routines execute strictly in whole-number integer space ($\mathbb{Z}$).

2.2 Boundary Limits and Overflow Handling

To preserve zero-dependency compatibility across all ECMAScript environments, FinEngine bounds integer execution within:

$$|V_{\text{scaled}}| \le \text{Number.MAX\_SAFE\_INTEGER} = 2^{53} - 1 = 9,007,199,254,740,991$$

For BDT ($k = 2$), this provides deterministic capacity up to:

$$V_{\text{max}} \approx 90,071,992,547,409.91 \text{ BDT} \quad (\approx 90.07 \text{ Trillion BDT})$$

Calculations exceeding this boundary raise a deterministic runtime exception rather than degrading into floating-point approximation.

2.3 Deterministic Rounding Schemes

When division or compound multiplication produces fractional sub-units, FinEngine utilizes:

  1. Half-Away-From-Zero (Commercial Rounding): Standard for consumer debt and retail installment schedules.
  2. Half-To-Even (Banker's Rounding): Applied to high-frequency ledgers to eliminate upward statistical drift across multi-account reconciliation.
Section 3

Actuarial Amortization and Terminal Balancing

3.1 Reducing-Balance Annuity Equation

FinEngine implements true reducing-balance loan amortization:

$$EMI = P \cdot \frac{r(1 + r)^n}{(1 + r)^n - 1}$$

Where:

  • $P \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is the principal scaled in Poisha ($P = \text{Amount} \times 100$).
  • $r \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the monthly interest rate ($r = \frac{\text{Annual Rate}}{12 \times 100}$).
  • $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is the total duration in months.

3.2 Discrete Allocation Mechanics

For each discrete installment period $t \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$:

$$I_t = \text{round}(B_{t-1} \cdot r)$$
$$P_t = EMI - I_t$$
$$B_t = B_{t-1} - P_t$$

Where $B_0 = P$.

3.3 The Terminal Reconciliation Rule

Because periodic rounding rounds interest to integer sub-units, minor variances ($\pm \Delta$ Poisha) can emerge between original principal and total principal deductions:

$$\sum_{t=1}^n P_t \neq P$$

FinEngine enforces strict ledger reconciliation at terminal period $t = n$:

$$P_n = B_{n-1}$$
$$EMI_n = P_n + I_n$$
$$B_n = B_{n-1} - P_n \equiv 0$$

This guarantees that the loan closes with an exact zero balance ($B_n \equiv 0$) without lingering fractional Poisha.

Section 4

Advanced Actuarial Solvers

4.1 Internal Rate of Return (XIRR) Solver

For non-uniform cash flows, FinEngine solves the net present value relation:

$$NPV(R) = \sum_{i=1}^m \frac{C_i}{(1 + R)^{\frac{d_i - d_0}{365}}} = 0$$

Roots are identified using an accelerated Newton-Raphson iterative solver:

$$R_{k+1} = R_k - \frac{NPV(R_k)}{NPV'(R_k)}$$
  • Convergence criteria: $|\Delta R| < 10^{-7}$
  • Maximum iteration ceiling: 100 cycles, with automatic bisection fallback to prevent client CPU lockup.

4.2 Debt-Burden Ratio (DBR) Validation

To support responsible lending standards, @finengine/math evaluates localized borrower affordability:

$$DBR = \frac{\sum EMI_{\text{existing}} + EMI_{\text{proposed}}}{Y_{\text{net monthly}}} \times 100$$

Transactions are checked against regulatory caps (such as central bank guidelines where $DBR \le 40\%$) via client-side stress testing routines.

Section 5

Local-First and Zero-Knowledge Architecture

FinEngine operates entirely within the user's local browser runtime:

  • Zero External Telemetry: Computation runs locally inside V8, SpiderMonkey, or JavaScriptCore.
  • Data Sovereignty: Financial figures, borrower profiles, and ledger lines are never transmitted over external networks.
  • Offline Resiliency: Core calculations function without internet connectivity, ensuring consistent reliability for offline accounting systems.
Section 6

Published Monorepo Surface and Pipeline

Released Packages (v0.3.0)

  • @finengine/core: Integer conversion engine, localized BDT formatting (3-2-2 grouping), and double-entry balance validation.
  • @finengine/math: Reducing-balance amortization, APR and XIRR solvers, and DBR assessment logic.
  • @finengine/ui: Unstyled UI components and KPI helpers (makeMoneyKpi, makeRepaymentSummary).

Future Research Modules (CFSBR Lab Pipeline)

  • @finengine/risk: Altman EM Z-score, DSCR and ICR stress testing, and NPL proxy modeling.
  • @finengine/microfinance: Transparent APR-to-flat conversion matrices and regulatory disclosure logic.
  • @finengine/ratios: Financial statement ratio stacks and 3-step / 5-step DuPont analysis.
  • @finengine/markets: Capital market return helpers and portfolio variance tools.
  • @finengine/tax: NBR-compliant TDS/VDS and salary tax calculation primitives.
