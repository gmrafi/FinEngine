Abstract

Modern financial technology interfaces increasingly offload real-time computational workloads to client-side runtimes. However, execution environments built on standard ECMAScript engines rely on IEEE-754 double-precision floating-point arithmetic, which introduces non-deterministic representation drift in fractional calculations. In statutory accounting, compounding debt repayment models, and high-frequency digital ledgers, this drift compounds non-linearly across time horizons, violating basic audit trail reconciliation standards.

This paper specifies the computational architecture of FinEngine, an open-source, non-commercial deterministic mathematical library developed under the Centre for Fintech and Strategic Business Research (CFSBR). FinEngine implements an integer-scaled monetary sub-unit arithmetic model, formalizes localized currency primitives for emerging markets—specifically the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT) and its atomic sub-unit (Poisha)—and introduces zero-knowledge, 100% client-side calculation pipelines for loan amortization schedules, internal rate of return (XIRR) solvers, and debt-burden stress testing.

Keywords: Deterministic computation, IEEE-754 binary64, financial technology, Bangladeshi Taka (BDT), integer-scaled arithmetic, client-side computing, XIRR solver, loan amortization, zero-knowledge architecture.

Section 1

The Numerical Pathology of Client-Side Web Runtimes

1.1 IEEE-754 binary64 Floating-Point Drift

ECMAScript standardizes numerical calculation on the IEEE-754 double-precision floating-point format (binary64). Numbers are allocated 64 bits of storage divided into three discrete fields:

Field Bits Description
s 1 Sign bit: 0 = positive, 1 = negative
e 11 Biased exponent (bias = 1023)
m 52 Fraction/Significand (53 bits with implicit leading 1)

Under this schema, numbers are evaluated as:

V = (-1)s × (1.m)2 × 2e - 1023
(1.1)

Because fractional values are expressed as sums of negative powers of two (2-1 = 0.5, 2-2 = 0.25, 2-3 = 0.125, etc.), common decimal fractions cannot be represented exactly in finite base-2 expansion. For example, the decimal fractions 0.1 (1/10) and 0.2 (1/5) result in infinite repeating binary fractions:

0.110 = 0.00011001100110011...2
0.210 = 0.0011001100110011...2
(1.2)

Truncating this infinite representation to 53 bits of significand generates an inexact representation in binary storage:

0.110 ≈ 0.100000000000000005551115123126
0.210 ≈ 0.200000000000000022204460492503
(1.3)

Evaluating simple addition in JavaScript yields the well-documented anomaly:

0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004 ≠ 0.3
(1.4)

1.2 Compounding Propagation in Loan Amortization

While fractional variance on the order of O(10-17) is negligible in graphical rendering or casual statistics, it is catastrophic in recursive financial operations. Consider a reducing-balance loan schedule over n periods, where the outstanding balance Bt is defined by the recurrence relation:

Bt = Bt-1(1 + r) - EMI
(1.5)

When evaluated under floating-point arithmetic, each iteration t ∈ {1, …, n} accumulates representation drift εt:

Bt* = Bt + ∑k=1t εk (1 + r)t-k
(1.6)

In multi-year amortization schedules (n ≥ 60 months), cumulative rounding drift ε causes the closing terminal balance Bn to fail to liquidate cleanly to zero (Bn ≠ 0.00). In statutory double-entry bookkeeping, a balance of +0.02 or -0.01 breaks immutable ledger parity, triggers audit violations, and complicates closing reconciliation.

2.1 Integer Sub-Unit Scaling

FinEngine represents all currency balances strictly as integer quantities of the currency's smallest statutory atomic unit. For the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT), the smallest atomic unit is the Poisha (1 BDT = 100 Poisha).

Definition 2.1: Atomic Poisha Scaling Mapping

The transformation bijection f: ℚ → ℤ is formalized as:

Vscaled = ⌊ V · 10k + 1/2 sgn(V) ⌋
(2.1)

Where:

  • V is the decimal monetary value.
  • k is the currency scale exponent (k = 2 for BDT).
  • sgn(V) preserves directional signs.

All internal intermediate computations (addition, subtraction, and discrete balance tracking) execute entirely within the closed domain of integers (ℤ).

2.2 Numerical Safety Boundaries

To maintain execution performance across all client engines without forced polyfills, FinEngine operates within the native ECMAScript safe integer boundary:

|Vscaled| ≤ Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER = 253 - 1 = 9,007,199,254,740,991
(2.2)

For BDT (k = 2), this ceiling provides safe deterministic headroom up to:

Vmax ≈ 90,071,992,547,409.91 BDT (≈ 90.07 Trillion BDT)
(2.3)

Any arithmetic transaction or ledger verification projected to exceed 253 - 1 triggers a deterministic boundary exception (SafeIntegerOverflowException) rather than decaying silently into floating-point approximation.

2.3 Deterministic Rounding Specifications

When division or compound multiplication leaves the integer domain, FinEngine employs Half-Away-From-Zero (Symmetric Rounding) for consumer-facing installment calculations, or Half-To-Even (Banker's Rounding / IEEE-754 default) for balanced institutional ledgers to prevent positive cumulative drift over large sample sizes.

Section 3

Computational Loan Amortization Models

3.1 Reducing-Balance Equated Monthly Installment (EMI)

In contrast to flat-rate interest calculations—which obscure actual borrowing costs and understate the effective annual rate—FinEngine enforces the actuarial reducing-balance annuity model:

EMI = P · [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n - 1]
(3.1)
Variable Type Definition
P Integer (ℤ) Principal loan amount in integer poisha (P = Principal × 100)
r Decimal (ℚ) Periodic monthly rate: r = Annual Rate / 1200
n Integer (ℕ) Total repayment months (n ≥ 1)

3.2 Discrete Iteration and Periodic Partitioning

For every discrete installment period t ∈ {1, 2, …, n}, the engine derives periodic interest accrual (It), principal liquidation (Pt), and closing balance (Bt) via strict integer truncation rules:

It = round(Bt-1 · r)
Pt = EMI - It
Bt = Bt-1 - Pt
(3.2)

Where B0 = P.

3.3 The Terminal Reconciliation Rule

Because discrete interest rounding rounds periodic values to the nearest whole poisha, the sum of principal portions naturally drifts from the starting principal by small integer amounts (± Δ poisha):

∑t=1n Pt ≠ P
(3.3)
Theorem 3.1: The Terminal Reconciliation Rule & Zero-Drift Closure

FinEngine resolves periodic drift through the Terminal Reconciliation Rule. In the final payment period (t = n):

Pn = Bn-1
EMIn = Pn + In
Bn = Bn-1 - Pn ≡ 0
(3.4)

This deterministic boundary constraint guarantees that the closing principal balance terminates identically at zero (Bn ≡ 0), completely preventing trailing poisha discrepancies in audited accounting ledgers.

Section 4

Advanced Solvers and Actuarial Engines

4.1 Internal Rate of Return (XIRR) Solver

For non-periodic, non-uniform digital transactions (such as merchant cash advances and micro-installments), FinEngine solves for internal rate of return via the generalized net present value relation:

NPV(R) = ∑i=1m Ci / (1 + R)(di - d0)/365 = 0
(4.1)

Where:

  • Ci is the cash flow at date di.
  • d0 is the initial disbursement timestamp.
  • R is the annualized discount factor.

Because closed-form solutions do not exist for m > 4, FinEngine uses a constrained Newton-Raphson root-finding iteration coupled with binary bisection fallbacks:

Rk+1 = Rk - NPV(Rk) / NPV'(Rk)
(4.2)
  • Convergence Threshold: |Δ R| < 10-7
  • Iteration Cap: Maximum 100 iterations. If convergence fails due to sign stagnation, the engine switches to bounded Brent-Dekker root bracketing, preventing runaway client CPU usage.

4.2 Debt-Burden Ratio (DBR) Stress Testing

To prevent predatory micro-lending and ensure compliance with central banking guidelines on responsible consumer lending, @finengine/math evaluates localized debt burden limits:

DBR = [(∑ EMIexisting + EMIproposed) / Ynet monthly] × 100
(4.3)

The engine validates transactions against statutory thresholds (e.g., DBR ≤ 40%) and provides deterministic sensitivity tables evaluating borrower risk under interest rate shocks.

Section 5

Architectural Topology: Local-First and Zero-Knowledge

FinEngine is structured as a client-side library to protect user data:

CLIENT BROWSER ENVIRONMENT
↓
User Financial Inputs(Income, Principal, EMI)
Local Form State(No Cloud Transmission)
↓
@finengine/coreFixed-Point Integer Engine · BDT Sub-Unit Parser · Ledger Verification
↓
@finengine/mathReducing Balance Amort. · XIRR / APR Solvers · DBR and Stress Testing
↓
@finengine/uiUI Micro-Components · Schedule and KPI Display
↓
X (Zero-Network Egress)
Figure 2: Zero-network client-side execution pipeline for financial telemetry privacy.
  • Zero-Knowledge Computation: Financial calculations execute entirely within the local host runtime (V8, SpiderMonkey, JavaScriptCore). No raw loan values, salaries, or accounting data cross the network boundary.
  • Offline-First Reliability: The engine runs without external API handshakes, keeping loan assessments, installment schedules, and merchant reconciliations fully operational in low-bandwidth or offline environments.
