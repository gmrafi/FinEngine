Case 1: The "Flat vs Reducing APR" Illusion

A microfinance lender advertises a "10% Flat Rate" on a 1-year BDT 50,000 loan. While it sounds cheaper than a 14% commercial bank loan, calculate the true statutory reducing APR.

• Advertised Flat Rate: 10.0% (Interest charged on initial 50k every month)
• True Reducing APR: ~18.46% APR
💡 Why? In a flat rate, you pay interest on money you have already repaid during the year. FinEngine automatically reveals the true effective APR.

Case 2: Laptop & Study Financing Plan

A student purchases an engineering laptop for BDT 85,000 on a 12-month 0% card installment or a 12% student micro-loan. How does the EMI impact a BDT 25,000 monthly allowance?

• Monthly EMI: BDT 7,552 / month
• Debt Burden Ratio (DBR): 30.2% of Income (Healthy range)
💡 Guideline: Financial prudence recommends keeping personal debt installments below 35% of reliable recurring cash inflow.

Case 3: The "Missing Poisha" in Bank Portfolios

When calculating daily compounding interest across 10,000 corporate accounts, standard floating-point numbers (`0.1 + 0.2 === 0.30000000000000004`) cause audit reconciliations to fail.

• Standard IEEE-754: 0.30000000000000004 (Drift: 5.5e-17)
• FinEngine Integer Poisha: Exact 30 Poisha (Terminal B_n === 0.00)
💡 Core Rule: FinEngine scales all calculations into discrete integer sub-units (Poisha: 1 BDT = 100 Poisha) to ensure 100% audit closure.
📈 Project Valuation Newton-Raphson

Case 4: Irregular Cashflows & XIRR

Traditional IRR assumes payments occur at equal intervals (e.g. exactly every 30 days). In real commerce, investments and revenues occur on irregular calendar dates.

• Simple ROI: Ignores precise timing and duration
• Actuarial XIRR: Day-count accurate annualized return
💡 Solver: FinEngine uses a hybrid Newton-Raphson solver with bisection guarantees to converge on exact yield rates.
